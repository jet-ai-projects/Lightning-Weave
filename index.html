Abstract

A core goal of efficient reasoning is to improve the accuracy–efficiency frontier. However, jointly improving reasoning accuracy and inference efficiency can be challenging, as the two objectives can favor different reasoning behaviors. Independently post-trained models already offer distinct strengths in accuracy and efficiency. We introduce Lightning Weave, a post-training framework that extracts and composes these independently learned capabilities in a single student through on-policy distillation. Each acquired capability is represented by the policy shift from the model before post-training to the resulting specialist. Lightning Weave combines aligned log-ratio shifts at shared student token states and uses Tilted-Target DOPD to convert the cached signals into a stable learning target. Each anchor pair scores the cached trajectories once, enabling subsequent student training without serving multiple live anchor models concurrently. Across diverse student models and benchmarks in mathematics and code, Lightning Weave substantially improves upon the base students and achieves a state-of-the-art accuracy–efficiency frontier. On Qwen3.5-4B, it raises HMMT 2025 accuracy from 59.2% to 64.0% with 10.7% fewer response tokens, and LiveCodeBench v5 accuracy from 41.7% to 54.2% with 9.6% fewer response tokens. Adjusting the relative strengths of the anchor signals yields a strong empirical accuracy–efficiency Pareto frontier.

01 · Method

Extract each skill. Weave it per token. Distill offline.

Accuracy and efficiency pull reasoning in different directions, so training for both at once is hard. Specialists that already learned each one exist. Lightning Weave transfers what they learned through the student's own outputs, so the anchors can differ from the student in size and family.

1

A capability is a policy shift

Pair each specialist π+ with its checkpoint before post-training π−. Their token-level log-ratio keeps what post-training changed and drops what the base already knew.

\[ r_i(a\mid s)=\log\frac{\pi_i^{+}(a\mid s)}{\pi_i^{-}(a\mid s)} \]
2

Weave the shifts at every token

All anchor pairs score the same student rollouts, so their shifts line up token by token. Shifts that agree reinforce each other, conflicts are settled locally, and the weights set the balance.

\[ r_{\mathbf w}(a\mid s)=\sum_{i=1}^{K} w_i\, r_i(a\mid s),\qquad \textstyle\sum_i w_i = 1 \]
3

Tilted-Target DOPD

The composed shift tilts the frozen behavior policy into one explicit target, and the student minimizes its KL to that target. The loss and gradient vanish once the student reaches it, which makes offline training stable.

\[ q_{\mathbf w}(a\mid s)=\frac{\pi_b(a\mid s)}{Z_{\mathbf w}(s)}\exp\!\Big(\frac{r_{\mathbf w}(a\mid s)}{\alpha}\Big) \]\[ \mathcal L(\theta)=\alpha\,\mathbb E_{s\sim d_{\pi_b}}\big[D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_\theta\,\|\,q_{\mathbf w})\big] \]
4

Score once, train the student alone

Online, two capabilities mean four anchor models served next to the student at every step. Here each pair scores the cached rollouts once, following Lightning OPD, and training needs only the student and the cached scores. One cache serves every weighting.

02 · Tilted target

Cached shifts need a destination.

Reusing DOPD's sampled-token objective on a fixed cache keeps pushing after the shift has been absorbed, because its KL penalty is estimated on cached actions that the student has moved away from. The tilted target gives training a fixed point. It matches online DOPD without serving any anchor.

04 · Frontier

Turn one weight, choose the operating point.

Each weight is one student trained from the same cache. More DECS weight shortens responses, and the middle weights keep or raise accuracy. The non-dominated students trace a Pareto frontier over the base, both single anchors and four other methods.
